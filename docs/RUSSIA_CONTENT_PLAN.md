# RUSSIA — content plan

Updated: 2026-09-09  
Branch: `country/russia`

This is the new-country production brief for JOURNEY ATLAS Russia. It contains editorial and visual-design intent only. It does not connect unapproved Hero, Scene, Taste, or Map assets to production and does not change `atlasPublished`.

## Page concept

**RUSSIA / ロシア**

Lead direction:  
オネガ湖の木造教会から、モスクワとサンクトペテルブルクの都市景観、ヴォルガ沿いのカザン、バルト海の砂丘、バイカル湖、アルタイ、レナ川、カムチャツカへ。ヨーロッパと北アジアにまたがる距離と地形の変化を地図で追うと、ひとつの国の中に複数の世界があることが見えてくる。

The page should reveal Russia through:
- northern wooden architecture and Lake Onega rather than a generic capital-only entrance
- Moscow and Saint Petersburg as distinct historic urban landscapes
- Kazan as a Volga/Tatar cultural layer
- the Baltic sand landscape of the Russian Curonian Spit
- Lake Baikal as an inland-water world
- Altai as a steppe-to-alpine mountain system
- the Lena River and periglacial rock formations of Yakutia
- Kamchatka as a volcanic Far East landscape
- long-distance movement and time zones as part of understanding the country itself

Current-safety editorial rule:
- As of 2026-09-09, Japan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs lists the Ukraine-border surrounding regions at Level 4 and the rest of Russia, including Moscow, at Level 3.
- JOURNEY ATLAS must not present the current page as a recommendation to travel now.
- Travel Scale and transport copy are therefore framed as geographic / future-trip understanding for a time when official travel advice has materially improved.
- Time-sensitive safety, international transport, payment, entry and border information must be refreshed again at implementation and final QA.

Boundary / geopolitics rule:
- Scene selection is limited to places inside internationally uncontested Russian territory.
- Crimea and Russian-claimed / occupied Ukrainian regions are not used as Country scenes, routes or itinerary examples.
- Map rendering follows the project's approved geographic source and must not invent or editorially redraw disputed boundaries.

## Hero

Working coordinate: 62.0676, 35.2244

Planned filename: `hero-kizhi-pogost-lake-onega.webp`

Visual identity:
- Kizhi Pogost clearly identifiable on the Lake Onega shoreline
- Church of the Transfiguration, Church of the Intercession and bell tower read as one ensemble
- the 22-cupola silhouette of the Transfiguration Church must remain structurally plausible
- wide Lake Onega water / meadow / sky context rather than a tight architectural portrait
- place the church ensemble on the right or centre-right so the left side remains a calm text safe area
- summer or very early autumn, restrained natural daylight
- no theatrical sunset, snowstorm, fantasy mist, exaggerated domes or invented village skyline
- no crowds as primary subjects
- photo 6 : quiet watercolor 4

Role:
- strongest single visual entrance into Russia
- immediately identifiable architecture, but quieter and less expected than Red Square
- introduces the north-western lake-and-wooden-architecture layer before the page expands across the country
- Hero remains visually distinct from all 8 Scenes

## Locked 8-scene selection

### S01 — Moscow / Kremlin & Moskva River

Working coordinate: 55.7540, 37.6204  
Planned filename: `moscow-kremlin-moskva-river.webp`  
Content ID: `moscow-kremlin-moskva-river`

Scene identity:
- broad river-side view of the Moscow Kremlin
- red Kremlin wall and several characteristic towers readable at realistic scale
- Moskva River foreground provides spatial depth
- historic skyline rather than a close-up of a single cathedral

Why it is included:
Moscow is the historic and political centre, but the river-facing Kremlin gives it a geographic role instead of reducing the capital to Red Square iconography.

Working coordinate: 59.9500, 30.3183

### S03 — Kazan / Kremlin & Kazanka

Working coordinate: 55.7995, 49.1065

## Signature Facts

Exactly 3:

1. **11の時間帯**
   - Russia currently uses 11 time zones from UTC+2 through UTC+12 and does not observe seasonal clock changes in 2026.
   - This is used to explain scale, not as a decorative trivia count.

2. **バイカル湖 — 最深1,700 m**
   - UNESCO describes Lake Baikal as the world's deepest lake and estimates its age at about 25 million years.
   - The card number remains 1,700 m; the age belongs in supporting copy rather than stacking multiple numbers.

3. **キジ島・顕栄聖堂 — 22のドーム**
   - UNESCO describes the 37 m-high Church of the Transfiguration as topped by 22 bulbous cupolas.
   - The fact explains why the Hero silhouette is visually distinctive.

Do not substitute generic counts such as number of federal subjects, national parks or World Heritage properties.

## Taste

Proposed four dishes:

1. **ペリメニ / Pelmeni**
   - small filled dumplings
   - visual identity: one plain plate, dumplings clearly separated enough to read their shape
   - no tea, vodka, cutlery, linen, sauce bowls or table props

2. **シチー / Shchi**
   - cabbage-based soup
   - one simple bowl; broth, cabbage and modest ingredients readable
   - no bread basket, spoon, napkin or rustic-table background

3. **ビーフ・ストロガノフ / Beef Stroganoff**
   - sliced beef in a creamy / savoury sauce
   - one plate only
   - accompaniment, if used, must stay on the same plate and not become a second dish
   - avoid glossy restaurant advertising treatment

4. **ピロシキ / Pirozhki**
   - filled baked or fried buns / pastries
   - one simple plate with a small number of pieces
   - no tea glass, samovar, cloth, flowers or bakery interior

Selection rationale:
- dumpling / soup / meat dish / filled pastry
- four clearly different silhouettes and textures
- avoids using borscht as a Russia-defining dish, where cultural attribution would create unnecessary ambiguity
- national familiarity plus everyday-food range

## Seasons

Because the country spans many climate zones, these are broad travel-reading categories rather than uniform national weather claims.

1. **4〜5月**
   - European cities begin to move into spring while northern, Siberian and mountain regions can retain snow or difficult ground conditions.
   - Avoid claiming one national “best spring season.”

2. **6〜8月**
   - long daylight supports Karelia, Altai, Baikal and many river / lake landscapes
   - high-latitude and taiga regions can also have insects and rapidly changing weather
   - region-specific access still needs current checks

3. **9〜10月**
   - autumn colour becomes a strong visual layer in forest zones
   - temperatures fall quickly in Siberia, mountain areas and the Far East
   - early and late autumn must not be treated as equivalent everywhere

4. **11〜3月**
   - snow and frozen landscapes are visually characteristic in many regions, but cold, daylight, road conditions and ice vary enormously
   - winter travel should be described as a separate planning mode rather than a standard extension of a city trip
